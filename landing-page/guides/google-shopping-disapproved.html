Why Google Shopping products get disapproved

  1. Account-level policy issues
  2. Missing product data
  3. Incorrect brand
  4. GTIN / identifier issues
  5. Variant issues (color / size)
  6. Misrepresentation

1. Account-level policy issues

These are not “one title is wrong.” Google may limit or suspend the whole Merchant Center for policy (for example misrepresentation, counterfeit signals, or repeated violations). Cleaning a single SKU won’t clear it.

What to do: read the account notification in Merchant Center first; appeal or fix account compliance there.

2. Missing product data

Required Shopping attributes missing—image, price, availability, title, link—often show as item issues. Catalogs imported from 1688 → Shopify are especially prone to empty or placeholder fields.

3. Incorrect brand

Using a factory / supplier name as brand (or leaving brand blank when Google expects one) is a common disapproval path for dropship and 1688-sourced catalogs.

What to do: use the brand shoppers recognize for your storefront—not the 1688 vendor string.

4. GTIN / identifier issues

Fake or recycled barcodes are a hard risk. If you don’t have a real GTIN, use the compliant identifier exists = no path with a real brand—don’t invent codes.

5. Variant issues

Color and size that don’t match the offer, or “attribute walls” stuffed into titles, confuse matching and policy checks. One clear color + one size per offer beats a supplier-style title dump.

6. Misrepresentation

Landing page, price, or product claims that don’t match the Shopping listing. Often account-level when systemic.

How to tell whether it’s an account or feed problem

  • Many SKUs, same policy code, or account banner → start with account.
  • Scattered item issues (title, brand, GTIN, image) → start with feed / catalog data.
  • Mixed → fix account blockers first, then clean the feed.
